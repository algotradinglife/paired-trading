Confidence tercile boundaries determined per-pool by signal density. n is balanced between high/mid by design; low has more signals because boundary terciles are asymmetric.

Interpretation

  • High-confidence signals often correspond to very clean textbook divergence patterns — but these are also the ones most likely to have been "already partially priced in" by the time the bar confirms. The divergence is obvious to all observers.
  • Mid-confidence signals are the sweet spot: the divergence is real (pattern-confirmed) but not obvious, giving a better fade price at entry.
  • Low-confidence signals are competitive on EV (better than high), likely because the lower n threshold brings in more genuine divergences with noisy context that doesn't affect the underlying price dynamics.
